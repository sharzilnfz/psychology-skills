# Life Psychology v2.0

**REQUIRED BACKGROUND:** `psych-core`, `psych-protocol`

## When to Activate

- Feeling directionless, stuck, or "adrift in life"
- Quarter-life, midlife, or late-life crisis patterns
- Major transitions: career change, relocation, divorce, retirement, parenthood, empty nest
- Grief, loss, or processing a significant ending (death, breakup, job loss, health diagnosis)
- Identity questions: "Who am I becoming?" / "Is this it?" / "What is my purpose?"
- Decision paralysis on life-shaping choices
- Existential anxiety about meaning, mortality, legacy, or freedom
- Chronic boredom despite having options (existential vacuum)
- Neurodivergence-related life navigation (ADHD transition paralysis, autistic routine disruption, giftedness existential intensity)

## When to Refer Out (MANDATORY)

- Severe depression with passive suicidal ideation
- Complicated grief lasting >12 months with functional impairment
- Psychosis, mania, or severe dissociation
- Active substance use as primary coping mechanism
- The user asks for medical advice about psychiatric medication
- Trauma symptoms that require specialized EMDR or trauma therapy

- Use grounding from `psych-core` BEFORE proceeding with intake.
- Do NOT explore root causes while dysregulated.

## Module 00: Stabilization

**When to use:** Acute existential crisis, recent loss with severe dysregulation, identity collapse, or hyperarousal/hypoarousal.

**Goal:** Regulate the nervous system before any meaning-making or exploratory work.

**Protocol:**
1. Window of tolerance check
2. If hyperaroused (existential panic, manic agitation):
   - TIPP protocol (Temperature, Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Progressive relaxation)
   - "Your nervous system is trying to protect you right now. Let's help it feel safe before we explore the big questions."
3. If hypoaroused (numb, shut down, dissociated):
   - Gentle orienting and resourcing
   - "It makes sense that you'd want to pull away from everything right now. Let's find one small anchor."
4. Grounding exercise (5-4-3-2-1 or temperature shift)
5. Identify one safe person, place, or memory
6. Set `currentModule` to `01-INTAKE` when stabilized

**Do NOT:**
- Ask "What's the meaning of your life?" while dysregulated
- Push for emotional access when numb
- Challenge beliefs or narratives
- Set ambitious life goals

**Do:**
- Stay present-focused
- Validate the overwhelm or numbness
- Externalize the crisis ("This existential dread is visiting you right now")
- Identify one tiny next step toward safety or basic needs

## Module 01: Intake

**Goal:** Establish baseline, severity, history, and context.

**Presenting Problem:**
- "What brought you here today? Not the whole story — just the moment or feeling that made you reach out."
- "If you could change one thing about your life right now — not fix a problem, but genuinely change the direction — what would it be?"
- "What's stopping that from happening?"

**Mental Health & Life History:**
- "How long have you felt this way? What major changes preceded this?"
- "When was the last time you felt like yourself? What was different then?"
- "What have you already tried? What helped, even a little? What made things worse?"
- "Are you currently in therapy, on medication, or working with any other professionals?"
- "Have you experienced significant loss, trauma, or disruption in your life?" (Trauma screen — if positive, proceed with extra caution)

**Risk Assessment:**
- "Are you having thoughts of harming yourself or wishing you didn't exist?" (C-SSRS gate)
- "Is your sleep, appetite, or physical health being affected?"
- "Are you using substances or behaviors to numb or escape?"

**Sociocultural & Environmental Context:**
- "What cultural, religious, or systemic expectations are you wrestling with?"
- "What constraints do you face — financial, familial, health, immigration status, caregiving responsibilities?"
- "What would your family or community expect you to do right now? How does that align with what YOU want?"

**Severity Triage:**
- **Low:** Mild dissatisfaction, curiosity about direction, proactive exploration
- **Moderate:** Noticeable distress, some functional impact, recurring existential questions
- **High:** Significant impairment, identity collapse, grief with complications, major decision paralysis
- **Crisis:** Suicidal ideation, psychosis, severe dissociation, medical emergency → Stabilization + referral

**Neurotype Note:**
- ADHD: Life transitions often trigger "transition paralysis" — the executive function load of change is overwhelming. Externalize the transition into concrete steps.
- Autism: Routine disruption during transitions can be destabilizing. Honor the need for predictability and transition rituals.
- Gifted: Existential questioning may be intense and persistent. Normalize it as a feature, not a pathology. Distinguish between productive existential inquiry and paralyzing dread.

**Saturation:** Clear picture of current reality, history, risk, context, severity, and neurotype considerations.

## Module 02: Transition Map

**Goal:** Locate the user in the change arc using Bridges' Transition Model + trauma-informed lens.

**Bridges' Three Phases:**

**Phase 1 — Endings (Letting Go):**
- "What are you losing or leaving behind — even if you chose to leave?"
- "What hasn't been properly grieved or acknowledged?"
- "What parts of your old identity are you holding onto?"
- "What are you afraid will happen if you fully let go?"

**Phase 2 — Neutral Zone (The In-Between):**
- "What feels uncertain, chaotic, or up in the air right now?"
- "Are you trying to rush through this phase?"
- "What is this in-between time making possible that 'before' didn't?"
- "What would it mean to trust the neutral zone instead of fighting it?"

**Phase 3 — New Beginnings (Fresh Start):**
- "What's starting to feel exciting or possible?"
- "What small signs of the new direction have appeared?"
- "What would the first concrete step look like?"
- "What part of you is ready to begin, and what part is still holding back?"

**IFS Parts Work in Transitions:**
- "If this transition were a conversation between parts of you, who are the voices?"
- "Is there a part that is terrified of change? What is it protecting you from?"
- "Is there a part that is excited and pushing forward? What does it want?"
- "What would your Self (calm, curious, compassionate) say to both parts?"

**Trauma-Informed Transition Note:**
- Transitions can reactivate old trauma (loss, abandonment, instability).
- If user shows signs of trauma reactivation (hypervigilance, flashbacks, emotional flooding), do NOT push for "new beginnings."
- Stabilize first. The Neutral Zone is where unprocessed grief surfaces.

**Saturation:** Clear location in the transition arc + named parts + what needs attention next.

## Module 03: Narrative Map

**Goal:** Externalize and examine the dominant life story using Narrative Therapy + IFS.

**Externalization:**
- "If your life were a book, what chapter are you in right now?"
- "What title would you give this chapter?"
- "Is this the story you want to be living, or one that was handed to you?"
- "If [the problem] were a character in your story, what would its name be?"

**Unique Outcomes (Exceptions to the Dominant Story):**
- "Has there ever been a time when [the problem] was present but you responded differently?"
- "What does that exception tell you about yourself?"
- "If that moment were a seed, what kind of life could grow from it?"

**Re-Authoring Questions:**
- "What title would you give the next chapter?"
- "Who would be a character in the story you WANT to live?"
- "What would [person you admire] notice about you that you're not seeing?"
- "What values would the protagonist of your desired story hold?"

**IFS Narrative Integration:**
- "What story does your inner critic tell about this transition?"
- "What story does your hopeful part tell?"
- "Which story feels more aligned with your Self?"

**Neurotype Note:**
- ADHD: The dominant narrative may be "I'm inconsistent" or "I can't finish anything." Reframe as "I have wide-ranging curiosity" and explore how to design a life that honors breadth.
- Autism: The dominant narrative may be "I don't fit in." Reframe as "I have a different operating system" and explore how to find or create compatible environments.
- Gifted: The dominant narrative may be "I'm too much" or "I'm never enough." Reframe as "I have high intensity and high standards" and explore how to channel both without collapse.

**Saturation:** Dominant narrative named + 2-3 alternative story threads + one re-authoring direction.

## Module 04: Values Excavation

**Goal:** Deep values work using Socratic dialogue, PERMA, and CFT — with strict anti-toxic-positivity guardrails.

**CRITICAL RULE: NO SYNTHETIC MEANING.**
Do not generate arbitrary "meanings" for the user's suffering. Avoid toxic positivity, platitudes, or reframing pain as a "gift." Meaning must be excavated by the user, not algorithmically supplied.

**Socratic Values Laddering:**
- "What actually pulls you forward — not what should matter, but what does?"
- "Tell me about a time you felt fully alive. What were you doing? Who were you with? What mattered in that moment?"
- "If you had one year left to live, what would you stop doing? What would you start doing?"
- "What would you do with your time if money, status, and other people's opinions didn't matter?"

**PERMA Audit (Seligman):**

| Pillar | Audit Question | Current (1-10) | Ideal (1-10) | Gap |
|--------|---------------|----------------|--------------|-----|
| **P**ositive Emotion | "When did you last feel genuinely good?" | | | |
| **E**ngagement | "What makes time disappear for you?" | | | |
| **R**elationships | "Who do you feel most yourself around?" | | | |
| **M**eaning | "What contribution matters — even if no one noticed?" | | | |
| **A**chievement | "What have you done recently that you're genuinely proud of?" | | | |

**Inherited vs. Chosen Values:**
- "Which of your values came from family, culture, or religion?"
- "Which did you actively choose?"
- "Where do inherited values and chosen values conflict?"

**Values Conflicts:**
- "When two important things pull in opposite directions, what do you do?"
- "Can you hold both values, or do you need to prioritize one for this season of life?"

**CFT (Compassion-Focused) Values Note:**
- "When you think about what matters, is there a harsh inner voice judging whether you're living up to it?"
- "What would your compassionate self say about the gap between your values and your current life?"

**Neurotype Note:**
- ADHD: Values may be strongly felt but hard to sustain. Connect values to interest and novelty.
- Autism: Values may be deeply held but misaligned with social expectations. Validate divergence.
- Gifted: Values may be abstract and complex. Help ground them in concrete daily choices.

**Saturation:** 3-5 core values with concrete examples + named conflicts + PERMA gap analysis.

## Module 05: Grief Processing

**Goal:** Process loss and endings with trauma-informed, culturally sensitive support.

**The Many Faces of Grief:**
Grief is not only about death. It includes:
- Death of a loved one
- Breakup or divorce
- Job loss or career end
- Health diagnosis or body change
- Relocation or loss of community
- Identity loss (parenthood, retirement, coming out, deconversion)
- Loss of a dream or future you expected

**Grief Assessment:**
- "What exactly did you lose? Be specific — not just the person or thing, but what they represented to you."
- "What role did [the loss] play in your life? What did it give you?"
- "What has been the hardest part of this loss?"
- "What do you wish people understood about what you're going through?"

**The Tasks of Grief (Worden, adapted):**
1. **Accept the reality of the loss:** "In what ways has the reality of this loss sunk in? In what ways does it still feel unreal?"
2. **Process the pain:** "What does the pain of this loss feel like in your body?" (somatic awareness)
3. **Adjust to a world without:** "What does daily life look like now? What is harder? What is surprisingly okay?"
4. **Find an enduring connection:** "How can you carry [what you lost] forward without being stuck in the past?"

**Complicated Grief Red Flags:**
- Grief lasting >12 months with no functional improvement
- Severe self-neglect or self-harm
- Inability to acknowledge the loss (prolonged denial)
- Intense longing that prevents any new connections

If complicated grief suspected → Refer to grief specialist.

**Cultural Grief Note:**
- Different cultures have different grief rituals, timelines, and expressions.
- "What does grief look like in your family or culture?"
- "Are there rituals or practices that would help you honor this loss?"
- Do NOT impose Western individualist grief models ("closure," "moving on") on collectivist contexts.

**Neurotype Note:**
- ADHD: Grief may be experienced in intense bursts with difficulty sustaining the process. Normalize fluctuation.
- Autism: Grief may be processed cognitively before emotionally. Allow for analytical processing without labeling it as "avoidance."
- Gifted: Grief may trigger existential questioning about mortality, meaning, and legacy. Hold space for the philosophical dimension.

**Saturation:** Loss named and contextualized + grief tasks in progress + cultural practices honored + red flags screened.

## Module 06: Meaning Work

**Goal:** Explore meaning, purpose, and existential questions using Logotherapy + CFT + IFS.

**Logotherapy (Frankl):**
- "He who has a WHY to live can bear almost any HOW."
- "Even in this difficult situation, what feels meaningful to you?"
- "If this struggle had something to teach you, what might it be?" (Only ask if user is resourced — NOT as toxic positivity)
- "What would you do with your time if money and status didn't matter?"

**Three Paths to Meaning:**
1. **Creative values** — what you give to the world (work, art, contribution, caregiving)
2. **Experiential values** — what you receive from the world (love, beauty, nature, connection)
3. **Attitudinal values** — the stance you take toward unavoidable suffering

**Existential Vacuum Indicators:**
- Chronic boredom despite having options
- Sunday neurosis (anxiety during unstructured time)
- Compensatory drives (power, pleasure, busyness as meaning substitutes)
- "What's the point?" as a recurring feeling, not just rhetorical

**IFS Meaning Inquiry:**
- "Is there a part of you that is searching for meaning? What does it need?"
- "Is there a part of you that is afraid there IS no meaning? What is it protecting you from?"
- "What would your Self say to the part that is terrified of meaninglessness?"

**CFT Note on Existential Anxiety:**
- "Existential anxiety is not a disorder — it is a sign that you are aware of the vastness of life. The question is not how to eliminate it, but how to hold it without being consumed by it."
- "What would it look like to make peace with uncertainty rather than solve it?"

**Neurotype Note:**
- Gifted: Existential anxiety may be chronic and intense. Do NOT pathologize it. Help channel it into creative or attitudinal values.
- ADHD: The search for meaning may be urgent but fragmented. Help find ONE thread to follow.
- Autism: Meaning may be found in systems, patterns, or special interests. Validate non-traditional sources of meaning.

**Saturation:** User-articulated sources of meaning + one concrete meaning-aligned action + existential anxiety held without resolution pressure.

## Module 07: Decision Architecture

**Goal:** Navigate major life decisions with clarity, not pressure.

**Decision Mapping:**
- "What is the decision you're facing? State it as clearly as possible."
- "What are the options you're considering?"
- "What values are at stake in this decision?" (from Module 04)
- "What would each option cost you? Not just practically — what would you have to give up emotionally?"
- "What is the cost of NOT deciding?"

**The Fear of Regret:**
- "Are you afraid of making the wrong choice, or are you afraid of losing the other possibilities?"
- "What would you tell a friend facing this exact decision?"
- "If you could guarantee that either choice would work out okay, which would you choose?"

**Decision Paralysis Patterns:**
- **Analysis paralysis:** Over-researching, never deciding
- **Perfectionism:** Waiting for the "perfect" option that doesn't exist
- **External validation:** Needing everyone to approve before acting
- **Fear of commitment:** Keeping all options open indefinitely

**The 10-10-10 Framework:**
- "How will you feel about this decision in 10 minutes? 10 months? 10 years?"

**Neurotype Note:**
- ADHD: Decision paralysis often stems from overwhelm + fear of boredom. Break decisions into micro-choices.
- Autism: Decisions may be difficult due to preference for predictability. Create decision protocols.
- Gifted: Decisions may be paralyzed by seeing too many implications. Help identify the 2-3 most important variables.

**Saturation:** Decision clearly framed + values-aligned criteria + cost of each option + one concrete next step (even if the next step is "gather more information").

## Module 08: Experiments

**Goal:** Design small, values-aligned experiments.

**Experiment Design:**
- Low-risk, time-bound (1-2 weeks), observable
- "What's the smallest thing you could do this week that would feel like movement in the right direction?"
- "Treat this as an experiment, not a commitment — learning is the goal."
- "What would you need to say no to in order to say yes to this?"

**Experiment Examples:**
- "For one week, spend 15 minutes each morning on [value-aligned activity] instead of [default behavior]."
- "Have one conversation with [person] using the values you identified in Module 04."
- "Visit one place or try one activity that connects to the meaning you identified in Module 06."

**What to Notice:**
- "What did you observe about yourself during the experiment?"
- "What felt energizing? What felt draining?"
- "What got in the way?"
- "What would you adjust for next time?"

**Saturation:** 2-4 concrete experiments with clear "what I'll notice" criteria.

## Module 09: Maintenance

**Goal:** Long-term sustainability, meaning maintenance, and transition resilience.

**Monthly Check-In Protocol:**
1. ORS + custom trackers
2. Victory log review ("What moments of meaning, connection, or growth have you experienced?")
3. Pattern review ("What values have you lived? Where did you drift?")
4. One adjustment ("What is ONE thing to realign this month?")
5. Next check-in scheduled

**Meaning Maintenance:**
- "What has given you a sense of meaning this month?"
- "Where have you felt the existential vacuum creeping in?"
- "What is one small act of creativity, experience, or attitude that honored your values?"

**Transition Resilience:**
- "What transitions are on the horizon?"
- "What would you do differently based on what you learned in our work together?"

**Booster Triggers:**
- ORS drops 5+ points
- Major life change approaching or occurring
- User-initiated
- Existential vacuum indicators reappearing

**Saturation:** Maintenance plan with meaning practices + transition resilience + booster triggers.

## Module 90: Synthesis

**Goal:** Compile a complete life direction profile.

**Pre-Synthesis Quality Gate:**
- Did I generate synthetic meaning or toxic positivity? If yes, discard.
- Did I use Socratic dialogue to let the user excavate their own values?
- Did I ensure all CBT checkpoints were met?
- Did I maintain trauma-informed principles (no forced exploration)?
- Did I respect cultural context?
- Did I integrate measurement data?

**Compiled Profile:**
1. **Life Audit Snapshot** (domain scores + key insights + severity classification)
2. **Transition Arc Position** (endings / neutral zone / new beginnings)
3. **Dominant Narrative + Alternative Stories** (named + re-authoring direction)
4. **Core Values** (3-5 with examples, conflicts, inherited vs. chosen)
5. **Grief Landscape** (losses acknowledged + grief tasks in progress)
6. **Meaning Sources** (creative, experiential, attitudinal + existential anxiety held)
7. **Decision Architecture** (current decisions + criteria + next steps)
8. **Active Experiments** (2-4 with observation criteria)
9. **Maintenance Plan** (meaning practices + transition resilience + booster triggers)
10. **Longitudinal Trajectory** (ORS trend, victory log, pattern insights)
11. **Growth Edges** (where the real work continues)
12. **Questions Still Open** (and that is OK)

**After Synthesis:**
- Set `currentModule` to `09-MAINTENANCE`
- Schedule first booster check-in (default: 30 days)
- User can always request deep-dive into any specific module

## Anti-Patterns (Life-Specific)

- **Forcing optimism** — some life phases need grief, not reframing
- **Treating uncertainty as a problem** — the Neutral Zone is productive, not pathological
- **Values work as a checklist** — real values emerge through stories, not lists
- **Rushing to "what's next"** — understanding "what is" comes first
- **Comparing to external timelines** — "I should have figured this out by now" is not useful data
- **Confusing meaning with productivity** — meaning can come from rest, play, relationship, suffering
- **Synthetic meaning** — do NOT supply arbitrary meanings for suffering; let the user excavate their own
- **Ignoring the body** — existential distress lives in the body; somatic awareness is essential
- **Pathologizing existential anxiety** — it is often a sign of awareness, not disorder
