# Addiction Psychology — Framework Reference

## Stages of Change / Transtheoretical Model (Prochaska & DiClemente)
Change is not an event but a process through stages: precontemplation,
contemplation, preparation, action, maintenance. People cycle through stages
non-linearly. The intervention MUST match the stage — pushing action on
precontemplation creates resistance.

## Motivational Interviewing (Miller & Rollnick)
Spirit: partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation. The "righting
reflex" (urge to fix) is the enemy. Develop discrepancy between values
and behavior. Reinforce change talk (DARN-CAT). Roll with sustain talk.
Never argue, persuade, or confront.

## CBT — Cognitive Behavioral Therapy
Identify trigger-thought-craving-behavior-consequence chains. Challenge
permission-giving thoughts. Build alternative behavioral responses.
Chain analysis provides the data for intervention design.

## Relapse Prevention (Marlatt & Gordon)
Relapse is a process, not an event. High-risk situations + poor coping
= lapse. The Abstinence Violation Effect (AVE) converts lapses to relapses
via shame and all-or-nothing thinking. Prevention = identifying high-risk
situations + building specific coping responses + planning for lapses.

## DBT Distress Tolerance (Linehan)
Crisis survival skills for when emotional distress threatens to overwhelm:
TIPP (Temperature, Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Progressive relaxation),
ACCEPTS (distract with purpose), and radical acceptance.

## CFT — Compassion-Focused Therapy (Gilbert)
Shame drives addiction. The threat system (shame, self-criticism) fuels
the cycle. Building the compassionate self as an internal resource breaks
the shame-use cycle. "The part that uses is not bad — it's trying to
meet a need."

## SMART Recovery
Self-Management and Recovery Training. Evidence-based alternative to
12-step. Four-point program: building and maintaining motivation, coping
with urges, managing thoughts/feelings/behaviors, living a balanced life.

## Harm Reduction
Any movement toward less harm is progress, regardless of whether
abstinence is the goal. Meets people where they are. Recognizes that
access to full recovery resources is not equally distributed.
