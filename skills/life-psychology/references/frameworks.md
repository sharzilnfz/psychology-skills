# Life Psychology — Framework Reference

## Logotherapy (Frankl)
"He who has a WHY can bear almost any HOW." Meaning is found through three
paths: creative values (giving to the world), experiential values (receiving
from the world), and attitudinal values (stance toward unavoidable suffering).
The therapist does not supply meaning — the person discovers it.

## Narrative Therapy (White & Epston)
Externalize the problem ("the problem is the problem, not the person").
Identify the dominant story, find unique outcomes (exceptions), and
re-author toward a preferred story. The therapeutic conversation IS the
change mechanism.

## PERMA (Seligman)
Five pillars of wellbeing: Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships,
Meaning, and Achievement. Wellbeing is not the absence of suffering but
the presence of these elements. Audit each pillar 1-10 for gap analysis.

## Bridges' Transition Model
All change involves three phases: Endings (letting go of the old),
the Neutral Zone (in-between space — chaotic but creative), and
New Beginnings. People try to skip the Neutral Zone; it's where the
real work happens.

## IFS — Internal Family Systems (Schwartz)
The psyche has multiple "parts" (managers, firefighters, exiles) and a
core Self (curious, compassionate, calm). Parts are not pathological —
they are protective strategies. Work involves unburdening exiled parts
by accessing Self-energy.

## CFT — Compassion-Focused Therapy (Gilbert)
Three emotional regulation systems: threat (fight/flight), drive
(achievement), and soothing (safety). Most people are overactivated in
threat and drive, underactivated in soothing. Build the compassionate
self as an internal resource.

## DBT — Dialectical Behavior Therapy (Linehan)
Four skill modules: mindfulness (present-moment awareness), distress
tolerance (surviving crisis without making it worse), emotion regulation,
and interpersonal effectiveness. Key principle: dialectics — holding two
opposing truths simultaneously.
