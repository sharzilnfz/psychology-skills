# Module 50 — Repair Plan

## Purpose

Design concrete repair actions aimed at actually re-engaging the partner —
not just processing the conflict in the abstract.

## Method

- Specific behaviors, not vague intentions: not "be more attentive" but
  "ask about their day and listen for 10 minutes without problem-solving."
- Repair rituals: how to reconnect after conflict, specifically.
- Boundary work carried over from Module 40: what to protect, what to
  offer.
- A communication experiment: try the NVC format, once, in one named
  situation.
- What to do when an old pattern (a Horseman from Module 30) shows up
  again — a named pattern-interrupt, not just "try not to."

## Re-Engagement

The objective is to prepare the user to actually have this conversation
with their partner — not to have it here instead. Encourage stepping away
from the coaching conversation and initiating it in real life.

## Critical: Write to `commitmentLog` (closes v1's biggest gap in this niche)

Write the specific repair action to `state.json.commitmentLog` as
`pending`, with enough detail to actually check next time: what, with whom,
by when. v1 encouraged re-engagement but never structurally checked whether
it happened — Session Start's Commitment Review in `SKILL.md` now opens
every subsequent session by asking specifically whether this repair action
was attempted and what happened, before anything else. A repair plan that's
never followed up on is just a good intention.

## Saturation

3-5 specific, actionable repair commitments, at least one written to
`commitmentLog` in checkable form.

## On Completion

Standard `## Raw` / `## Synthesis` write.
